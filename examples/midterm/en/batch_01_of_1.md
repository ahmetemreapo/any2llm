Outcome and intervention

The outcome is the score on a 10-item test of research methods terms.
The practice activity takes place between the two tests.
Keep the outcome and the activity distinct when describing the method.

## Slide 2

What might affect the scores?

A second test may feel easier because the format is familiar.
Previous exposure to the terms might influence starting scores.
Use the same scoring rules for both classes.</file>

<page number="1">Designs with and without a
comparison group
Randomized pretest–posttest design
Assign participants or intact groups to conditions at random.
Measure the outcome before and after the intervention in each
condition. Random assignment helps reduce systematic
differences between conditions.
One-group pretest–posttest design
Measure one group before and after an intervention. There is no
comparison group. A change in scores may reflect the intervention
or something else that happened between the two measurements.</page>
<page number="2">Comparing existing groups
Quasi-experimental pretest–posttest design with
a nonequivalent comparison group
Use two existing groups without randomly assigning them to
conditions. Measure the same outcome in both groups before and
after the intervention. One group receives the intervention and the
other follows a comparison activity.
What the comparison can tell you
Compare the changes in the two groups, keeping baseline
differences and other explanations in mind. Existing groups can
differ in ways that affect the outcome, so the design alone does not
establish causation.</page>
